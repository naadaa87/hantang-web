HOW-TO

Is a jjimjilbang mixed? Where couples and friends meet

The baths are separated by sex, and the clothed jjimjilbang hall is shared. Where a mixed group splits up, how to find each other again without a phone, and where you can share the water.

5 MIN READ · Updated 2026-11-22

Partly. In a jjimjilbang (찜질방) the changing rooms and bath halls are single-sex, and the hall of heated rooms is shared by men and women in the cotton outfit the counter hands out. A couple or a mixed group of friends splits at the door, bathes apart and meets again in the hall. A plain neighborhood mogyoktang (목욕탕) has no shared hall, so there you meet outside. Our guide to bath types shows how to tell the two apart before you pay.

The route: together, apart, together

The split is law, not house custom. The facility standards under the Public Health Control Act (공중위생관리법), as posted by a Seoul district health office, say changing rooms and bath rooms must be run separately for men and women. The operator rules add that a sweat room shared by men and women may be used only in sweat clothing, which is why the outfit, the jjimjilbok (찜질복), is not optional. We read these rules on the health office's page and a legal reference site, not in the official gazette.

The same standards say sweat rooms, snack and service areas, and rest areas, sleeping rooms included, must be easy to see into and may not be built as closed rooms. The shared hall is public by design.

Before you split: three things to agree

  1. The ticket. Where a place sells a bath-only ticket and a ticket with the hall, everyone needs the second one. The counter may ask 찜질방 포함이요? (jjimjilbang pohamiyo?), "with the jjimjilbang hall?"
  2. A clock time. Phones stay in the locker while you bathe, so you cannot message from the bath hall. "When you are done" fails when one person showers for ten minutes and the other soaks for an hour. For a first visit, 40 to 60 minutes covers a full wash, a soak and one sauna sitting; that is our estimate from the routine in our rooms guide. Allow more for a body scrub.
  3. A place. You come out of different changing rooms, so "see you inside" is not enough. Pick something fixed, such as the snack counter, signed 매점 (maejeom).

Once you are in the outfit you can carry your phone into the hall; check the signs and never photograph strangers. Each person has their own key, at large places a wristband that also records purchases, and pays their own tab at the counter on the way out.

IF YOU CANNOT FIND EACH OTHER

Go back to the agreed spot and wait. To ask staff, say 일행을 찾고 있어요 (ilhaengeul chatgo isseoyo), "I am looking for my companion." To find the spot, ask 매점 어디예요? (maejeom eodiyeyo?), "Where is the snack counter?"

In the shared hall

This is where a group spends most of its time: the heated rooms, the ice room, the warm floor and the snack counter. A July 2026 explainer in the Korean online paper Wikitree describes it as the part of the visit that families, couples and friends share. In February 2026 the owner of a jjimjilbang near Hongdae told the Korea Economic Daily that foreign guests, most of them from China, came in groups more often than alone.

Keep the outfit on, and keep physical affection modest. The hall is a public lounge with children in it, and conduct rules are the facility's to set. In 2003, after cases of men groping women asleep in mixed rest areas at night, the daily Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that one Seoul jjimjilbang had posted a ban on physical contact between men and women, with removal for anyone who broke it. We found no national rule on couples' behavior, so read the notices on the wall.

At a 24-hour place, a pair who want to stay together at night sleep in the common sleeping area. A women-only sleeping room (여성전용수면실), where there is one, separates you again, and for a woman it is the safer choice. The standards we read do not say whether sleeping rooms must be divided by sex, and facilities differ. Agree on a morning meeting time before you say good night.

If you want to share the water

In an ordinary bath you cannot: the pools are single-sex and used without clothing. Three kinds of place work differently.

  • Spa water parks. Men and women share warm pools in swimsuits. The same building usually has single-sex baths and a jjimjilbang, often on separate tickets. Our spa water parks page listed 25 places in November 2026.
  • Family baths. In hot-spring towns, a gajoktang (가족탕) is a bath room rented by one family or couple.
  • Private saunas. A few venues rent a sauna room to one party by the session; one brand on our list has rooms for one to four people. See private baths and one-person scrub rooms.

Most cost more than a jjimjilbang ticket; the pages above give prices with the month we last saw them.

Same-sex pairs, children and other cases

Two friends of the same sex go through the whole visit side by side, the bath included. A parent with a child of the opposite sex has a legal line to watch: from age 4 (48 months) a child may not enter the bath or changing room of the other sex. For transgender and non-binary visitors we found no published policy. Our guide for families and other visitors sets out what is known in each case.

Pick a place that has a hall

If the point is to spend time together, choose a jjimjilbang, not a mogyoktang. At a mogyoktang you bathe on separate sides and meet at the entrance afterward, so set a time before you go in. On signs and map listings, look for the word 찜질방. A name ending in "sauna" can be either kind.
