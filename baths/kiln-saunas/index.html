Kiln saunas: hanjeungmak, bulgama and charcoal kilns

KILN SAUNAS

What the words mean

A hanjeungmak (한증막) is a low dome of stone and clay. A pine-log fire is lit inside and then raked out, and you go in while the walls are hot. People usually wear the jjimjil clothes and put a hemp sack or a towel over their head against the heat.

Bulgama (불가마, fire kiln) is what jjimjilbangs call their hottest room, often a clay kiln heated from outside. A sutgama (숯가마) is a real charcoal kiln: oak burns inside for days, the charcoal is taken out, and the kiln is opened for sweating while it cools, usually with a grill for pork or sweet potatoes next door.

Sweat rooms have a long history here: a hanjeungso, a sweat house, appears in the Annals of King Sejong in 1422.

Staying safe in the heat

  • Drink water first, start in the mildest room and come out after about five minutes.
  • Cover your skin and head with a towel, and take off metal jewellery and glasses, which heat up fast.
  • Step out at once if you feel dizzy. Skip the hot rooms if you are pregnant, have heart or blood-pressure problems, or have been drinking.
  • Countryside charcoal kilns are easiest to reach by car or taxi, and some close one or two days a week.
