title: Do you have to be naked in a Korean bathhouse? description: Yes in the bath hall, no in the jjimjilbang hall. Where you undress, where the cotton outfit is required by law, why swimsuits stay in the locker, and what to book if nudity is not for you. tag: HOW-TO ---

Yes, in the wet area. In the bath hall of a Korean bathhouse, the tiled room with the showers, pools and scrub tables, everyone is naked. Swimsuits and underwear are not worn, and the hall is for one sex only. In a jjimjilbang (찜질방), the shared hall of heated rooms, it is the reverse: everyone wears the cotton outfit, and in sweat rooms shared by men and women the law requires it. If you cannot undress in front of strangers, you can skip the bath hall or book a private room.

A neighborhood mogyoktang (목욕탕), a plain bathhouse, stops at the third row: it has no clothed hall. Our first-visit guide walks through every step.

What the law says, and what it leaves out

Bathhouse operators follow a list of duties in the enforcement rules of the Public Health Control Act (공중위생관리법). We read the version amended on 22 June 2022 on a Korean legal reference site, and an older 2019 summary from a Seoul district health office, not the official gazette. Three items matter here.

  • Clothes are required in mixed sweat rooms. An operator who runs a sweat room for men and women together must have guests put on sweat clothing (발한복) before they enter or use it.
  • Bath and changing rooms are single-sex. A child aged 4 (48 months) or older may not be let into the bath or changing room of the opposite sex.
  • Staff are the same sex as the bathers. In the bath, changing and sweat rooms, a men's bathhouse employs men and a women's bathhouse employs women. That includes the person who scrubs you.

The list has no line about swimsuits or underwear. Bathing naked is custom and house rule, not a legal duty, but it is a firm one: a July 2026 explainer in the Korean online paper Wikitree says swimsuits are not allowed in most ordinary public baths. We did not find an official text that gives the reason. The practical logic sits in the washing rule. You soap and rinse your whole body before you step into water that everyone shares, and nothing else goes into the pool, not even your towel. Our etiquette guide has the rest.

How first-time visitors feel about it

Unease is common, and Korean sources say so. The Wikitree explainer calls the bath area, where all clothes come off, the biggest hurdle for a first-time foreign visitor. In a 2019 episode on Korea.net, the government's English-language news site, a foreign participant says that undressing in a public bathhouse is a little embarrassing. The Korean host adds that some foreign friends stay away because of their religion.

A few facts may help. Everyone else in the room is undressed too. You may hold the small towel in front of you while you walk. Staring at other bathers is against bathhouse etiquette. Phones and cameras are banned in changing rooms and bath halls, and filming there is prosecuted as a sex crime.

Ways to go without undressing in front of strangers

Prices below come from our own facility pages, with the month we last saw them.

  • Use only the jjimjilbang hall. Buy the ticket that includes the hall, change into the outfit and go straight to the hot rooms. You will change in a single-sex changing room where other people are undressed, but the hall does not require you to enter the pools.
  • Choose a swimwear spa. Some large spas have pools that men and women share in swimsuits. Termeden in Icheon requires a swimsuit and swim cap indoors and outdoors (adult day ticket ₩54,000 in peak season, 2026-10). Club D Oasis in Busan has an outdoor seawater bath used in swimwear (Spa Pass ₩30,000, 2026-09).
  • Book a private room. Solo saunas and private baths rent a room to one person or one party. At SISU House in Seoul's Sinsa-dong, a 90-minute course in a one-person room with a bathtub and a Finnish sauna cost ₩79,000 (2026-05).
  • Book a one-person scrub shop. These shops offer seshin (세신), the Korean body scrub, in a private room. You undress in front of one attendant, not a hall full of people. In March 2026 Maeil Business Newspaper reported that about 40 percent of customers at DAMDA, a women-only shop near Hongdae, were foreign. Its basic package started at ₩83,000 (2025-08).
  • Rent a family bath. Hot-spring towns have the gajoktang (가족탕), a bath room rented by one family or couple. Our guide for families and other visitors lists examples in Busan and Sokcho.

Two options do less than they seem to. A women-only bathhouse removes men, not nudity. Hotel saunas are not always open to visitors; the same guide names two hotels that admit only guests and members. Privacy also costs more: the average adult ticket at an ordinary public bath in Seoul was ₩11,000 in July 2026, and the priced options above start at about ₩30,000.

If you decide to try the bath hall

The sequence is short. Undress at your locker, take the small towel and your toiletries, and walk to a shower station. Sit on the stool and wash from head to toe. From there you are doing what everyone else in the room is doing.

To pick a place, start with bathhouses near popular areas. If you would rather begin behind a closed door, the private baths and one-person scrub rooms list shows prices and how to book.
