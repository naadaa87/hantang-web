title: Busan sea-view spas: six places, and where you need a swimsuit description: Six spas and jjimjilbangs on Busan's coast, checked in November 2026: which zones need a swimsuit, which are naked single-sex baths, what they cost, and which one is for hotel guests only. tag: PLACES ---

Busan has baths by the water from Songdo in the west to Gijang in the east, and the confusing part is what to wear. In short, the sea view usually comes with clothes on. Outdoor pools and rooftop baths are mixed and need a swimsuit. The lounges of a jjimjilbang (찜질방) are used in the house uniform. The single-sex bath halls are used naked, as everywhere in Korea. Below are six places we could confirm as open in November 2026; one turned out to be for hotel guests only. Our in-person check is still pending, so everything here comes from official pages, Korea Tourism Organization (KTO) data, booking pages and Korean press, read on November 27, 2026.

Haeundae: Club D Oasis, Dalmaji Hill Spa and a hotel spa

Club D Oasis (클럽디 오아시스) opened on July 5, 2023 in the LCT tower at the east end of Haeundae Beach, the daily Financial News reported. Its official guide sets the rules by floor. The fourth is a water park: swimwear, aqua shoes and a cap are required. The fifth has the single-sex bath halls and an outdoor bath, Cheongsudang (청수당): in a swimsuit you may get in, in the jjimjilbang uniform you may use only the foot bath. The sixth is the jjimjilbang, where the uniform is required and swimwear is not allowed.

The six-hour Spa Pass covers the jjimjilbang and the outdoor bath and costs ₩33,000; the all-day pass with the water park is ₩54,000 (November 1 to December 18, 2026). The bath halls and outdoor bath open 10:00–22:00, and the jjimjilbang closes at 21:00 or 21:30. Tuesdays are closed days through December 15. Exit 7 of Jung-dong Station (Line 2) is 490 m away, the site says.

Dalmaji Hill Spa (달맞이 힐스파) is the plain Korean option: a 24-hour jjimjilbang on the hill east of the beach with no swimsuit zone. The news site Wikitree reported on October 12, 2026 that its bath fee rose from ₩10,000 to ₩12,000 on October 1. The booking platform WAUG, which sells a foreigner-only ticket, says the bath halls close for cleaning from midnight to 06:00 and lists a rooftop foot bath. We found no official website, no confirmed jjimjilbang price and no published route from a subway station, so plan on a taxi.

Cimer (씨메르) is the outdoor spa at Paradise Hotel Busan, facing the beach. The hotel's page says it is a guest-only facility that outside customers cannot use. Guests pay ₩44,000 (₩22,000 for children from 49 months to elementary school) for one of four sessions between 08:00 and 21:50, and it closes on the fourth Wednesday of the month. Our own listing page still shows ₩44,000 as a non-guest price; go by the hotel's page. It states no dress rule; our listing records swimwear.

Gwangalli: Hotel Aqua Palace

Hotel Aqua Palace (호텔 아쿠아펠리스) stands on the Gwangalli beachfront road. KTO listing data, republished by the travel site K-Trip Tips and updated on November 11, 2026, says the jjimjilbang is open 24 hours and looks over Gwangalli Beach, and that the water is seawater hot-spring water rising from 816 m at 38°C. It credits the view to the jjimjilbang and the infinity pool, not to the bath hall. The hotel's page on the booking app Yeogi says the indoor water park is suspended, and that the lane pool requires a swimsuit and a swim cap. Our listing page recorded ₩12,000 for the sauna alone and ₩30,000 with the jjimjilbang in September 2026. The hotel's own site would not open, so neither is reconfirmed.

Songdo: Spa Haesurang

Spa Haesurang (스파해수랑) is in Songdo, on the west side of the city. According to its official site, the men's bath hall (third floor) and the women's (fourth) open 06:00–22:00, the fifth-floor jjimjilbang 08:00–21:00 and the rooftop open-air bath 10:00–21:00. The rooftop bath admits only swimsuits and rash guards, and aqua shoes are mandatory. The site says Namhang Bridge and the sea can be seen from the sauna, the jjimjilbang and the open-air bath.

Gijang: Water House at Ananti Cove

Water House (워터하우스) belongs to the Ananti Cove resort on the Gijang coast, north-east of Haeundae. The whole facility is a swimsuit zone: the official page says entry is in swimwear, and ordinary T-shirts and shorts are not accepted. Ananti describes the water as natural hot-spring water pumped from 600 m. From September to June it opens 09:00–20:00 on weekdays and until 21:00 on weekends and holidays, and it closes on the third Tuesday of the month.

It is the expensive one. Single-visit prices are published as an image we could not read today; a ten-visit adult voucher is ₩630,000, and our listing page recorded about ₩60,000–79,000 for a weekday visit in September 2026. Resort guests enter free. The resort's location page gives no subway or bus route, so ask before you set out.

Is it seawater or a hot spring?

Signs in Busan use three words: oncheon (온천, hot spring), haesutang (해수탕, seawater bath) and haesu oncheon (해수온천, seawater hot spring). Only the first is defined in law: water that rises from underground at 25°C or more and meets set standards, as our hot springs guide explains. We found no official definition of the other two. Club D Oasis calls its water a weakly alkaline silica hot spring and says the Ministry of the Interior and Safety designated it the eighth National Health Hot Spring (국민보양온천). Its page does not use the word seawater, though our listing page does. Spa Haesurang says only "clean seawater" and makes no hot-spring claim.

Which one suits you

  • No swimsuit, small budget. Dalmaji Hill Spa. For a hot-spring bath without the sea view, Hurshimchung in Dongnae is on the subway.
  • Children and swimsuits. Club D Oasis, or Spa Haesurang's rooftop, where the rules say there is no lifeguard and children need a guardian.
  • Tattoos. Club D Oasis and Spa Haesurang say guests with excessive tattoos may be restricted or refused, and Water House asks guests to cover them.
