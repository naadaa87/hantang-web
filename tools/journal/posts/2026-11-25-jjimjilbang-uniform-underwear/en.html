title: Do you wear underwear under a jjimjilbang uniform? description: No rule says either way, and Korean bathers do both. What the law covers, what a 2006 hygiene test found, how each choice plays out, and why a dry spare pair matters more than the choice. tag: HOW-TO ---

It is your choice. No Korean rule says what goes under the jjimjilbok (찜질복), the cotton T-shirt and shorts that a jjimjilbang (찜질방) hands you at the counter, and Korean bathers do both. The practical point is different: whatever you wear in the hot rooms ends up damp with sweat, so make sure a dry pair of underwear is waiting in your locker for the trip home.

What the rules cover, and what they leave to you

Bathhouse operators follow a list of duties under the Public Health Control Act (공중위생관리법). We read it on two Seoul district health office pages and a legal reference site, not in the official gazette. Two lines concern the outfit.

  • It must be worn. A sweat room shared by men and women may be used only in sweat clothing (발한복).
  • It must be laundered. Towels, gowns and rental clothing must be washed before they are given to a customer.

Nothing in the list mentions underwear. The text does not define sweat clothing either, so whether your own T-shirt would do is up to the facility, and the usual answer is no. In 2010 a reporter for the Korean health paper Medical Today phoned ten Seoul jjimjilbang and asked to wear personal clothes because of a skin condition. Two agreed. One place said personal clothes were fine only with the outfit on top.

In the single-sex bath hall the question does not come up, because nothing is worn there at all. See Do you have to be naked in a Korean bathhouse?

What Korean bathers do

We found no survey, only conversation, and it shows a split. In a 2010 thread on Clien, a Korean community board, a poster asked this exact question and got 29 replies. About a dozen said they keep underwear on, several said they wear nothing underneath, and the rest were jokes or side questions. A forum thread is not data, but the reasons people gave are useful.

Why people choose itThe outfit is shared and reused. The shorts are loose.One layer less in the heat. Underwear gets soaked with sweat anyway.
The catchThe pair you wore is wet when you leave.The rental outfit sits directly on your skin.
What to packA second, dry pair for the trip home.Nothing extra. The pair you arrived in stays dry in the locker.

The poster assumed that men go without and women keep underwear on, and other posters disputed it. For bras we found no rule and no figures either.

Where the dry pair comes in

You undress at your locker and bathe first. Then you dry off, put on the outfit, with or without underwear, and go to the shared hall. When you are done, you take the outfit off in the changing room, rinse in the bath hall, dry off and put on street clothes. Whatever was under the outfit is damp by then. Is the outfit clean?

The law requires a laundered outfit every time. How clean that is has been tested once that we could find, and long ago. In 2006 the Korea Consumer Protection Board, the body now called the Korea Consumer Agency (한국소비자원), tested rental outfits from 20 jjimjilbang in Seoul. It found general bacteria on the outfits from 17 of them, the paper Toyo Gyeongje reported that July. The board noted that many guests wore the outfits without underwear. It advised people with skin wounds, people with chronic conditions such as diabetes, and people who sweat heavily not to wear rental clothing. It also pointed out that the rule sets no limit on bacteria.

Four years later a Seoul district official told Medical Today that rental outfits were not inspected unless someone complained. We found no newer test, so we cannot say what the picture is in 2026.

IF YOUR SKIN IS BROKEN OR SENSITIVE

The board's advice was to skip rental clothing, which in a jjimjilbang means skipping the shared hall. If you go anyway, your own underwear puts one layer of your own under the rental shorts. If the outfit you are handed is damp or stained, give it back and ask for another. Our health guide lists who should take care with the heat itself.

When the outfit is soaked

We found no facility that publishes a rule on swapping a sweat-soaked outfit for a fresh one, and we could not confirm whether places charge for it. Ask at the counter that gave it to you: 찜질복 하나 더 주세요 (jjimjilbok hana deo juseyo), "one more outfit, please."

KorMedi, a Korean health outlet, wrote in September 2026 about gym wear, not jjimjilbang outfits, but its advice carries over: do not stay in sweat-soaked clothes.

You return the outfit with the towels on the way out. A lost towel or uniform cost ₩5,000 to ₩10,000 in early 2026, according to the same prices guide.

Sizes, small children and where to go next

At a large jjimjilbang the ticket usually includes the outfit, two small towels and the locker key. The counter may ask 찜질복 사이즈는요? (jjimjilbok saijeuneunyo?), "What size uniform?" You can answer 큰 걸로 주세요 (keun geollo juseyo), "a large one, please," or 작은 걸로 주세요 (jageun geollo juseyo), "a small one, please." More counter phrases are in our phrase guide.

Aquafield, a spa chain with branches in Hanam, Goyang and Anseong, says on its usage page (seen 2026-11) that children under 36 months are not given an outfit or towel. It rents an infant outfit for ₩5,000 plus a ₩1,000 deposit. With a toddler, ask before you pay.
