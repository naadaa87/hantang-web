Hurshimchung (허심청, also spelled Heosimcheong) is the large hot-spring bathhouse next to Hotel Nongshim in Dongnae, in the north of Busan. It opened in October 1991, takes 3,000 bathers at a time and has more than 40 pools. Entry is ₩15,000 on weekdays and ₩18,000 on weekends and public holidays (November 2026). The bath is open every day from 05:30 to 22:00, so it is a place for a day visit, not for sleeping. Our in-person check is still pending. What follows comes from Korea Tourism Organization (KTO) listing data, republished by the travel site K-Trip Tips, and from government and press reports.

Why the bath is in Dongnae

Dongnae is one of the oldest recorded hot springs in Korea. The KTO entry for a stone monument in the district says the first written record is in the Samguk Yusa (Memorabilia of the Three Kingdoms), which has a Silla chancellor bathing here. Our hot springs guide dates that visit to 683.

That monument, Onjeong Gaegeonbi (온정개건비), was set up in 1766 to honor a Dongnae magistrate who rebuilt the bath with separate rooms for men and women. It stands less than 200 m from Hurshimchung and is free to see, though closed on Mondays.

The modern spa town came with Japanese settlers. The Korea Herald wrote in 2023 that a Japanese merchant built a bathhouse here in 1907, that a tram line reached the town in 1915, and that many Japanese-owned baths of the time took only Japanese guests. Hurshimchung, the paper says, was built where one of those first Japanese-style bathhouses had stood. The Dongnae Tourist Hotel opened here in 1960. KTO data says it was renamed Hotel Nongshim in August 2002 and rebuilt with 240 rooms. The hotel and the bath both belong to Nongshim, the Korean food group.

On 1 October 2026 the Ministry of the Interior and Safety named the Dongnae district Korea's fourth "hot-spring city" (온천도시), after Chungju, Asan and Changnyeong in 2023. The Ministry counts about 2 million visits a year. It describes the water as silica-bearing and up to 70.3°C at the source, the second hottest in the country after Bugok.

What is inside

KTO data gives the floor area as 4,297 m². The pools are sorted by theme: a "longevity" bath (장수탕), a cypress-wood bath, a celadon bath, a cave bath and an open-air bath, among others. A citizen reporter who bathed there in October 2026 for the government's policy news site described several kinds of pools under a high ceiling, and a display of old photographs of the spa town inside the building.

The same listing calls Hurshimchung the largest hot-spring facility in Asia. We found no measurement behind that claim.

The bath halls are separate for men and women, and nobody wears anything in them, as in any Korean public bath. According to our listing, a jjimjil zone on the third floor costs extra; there everyone wears the house uniform. The listing also notes a German-style beer hall in the building. Leave the beer until after the bath.

How a visit runs

Take Busan subway Line 1 to Oncheonjang Station (온천장역); the name means "hot-spring place." A hotel booking page puts Hotel Nongshim five minutes from the station on foot. We could not confirm the best exit from an official source, so follow a map app. The address is 32 Oncheonjang-ro 107beon-gil, Dongnae-gu.

After the ticket desk, the order is the same as at any Korean bath: shoes in a locker, clothes in the changing room, a full wash at the showers, then the pools. The first-visit guide linked below goes through each step. We could not confirm what the ticket includes besides entry, or whether a scrub can be booked on the day, so carry a small wash kit.

The source water is far too hot to sit in, so it is cooled before it reaches the tubs. Signs at Korean springs list conditions the water is said to help; treat those as tradition, not as a promise. Our heat and health guide explains time limits and who should skip the hottest pools.

Around the bath

  • A free foot bath. The Dongnae open-air foot bath (동래온천 노천족욕탕) is about 170 m from Hurshimchung. Spring water runs from a dragon-head spout. KTO data updated in November 2026 has it open 10:00–17:00 and closed on Mondays, Tuesdays, 1 January and the Seollal and Chuseok holidays.
  • The crane and the tram. Near the entrance to the spa streets, The Korea Herald describes a replica streetcar and golden statues of an old woman and a crane. In the local legend, a woman with a limp follows a wounded crane to the spring and is cured.
  • Geumgang Park. The park lies at the foot of Geumjeongsan, which became Korea's 24th national park in 2025. The government reporter walked the park first, then took a bus to the baths.

Staying next door

Hotel Nongshim is connected to the bath, and KTO data says its guest rooms are supplied with hot-spring water. That is an option if you would rather bathe in private. When we looked at the Korean booking app Yeogi in November 2026, room plans came with either half-price Hurshimchung tickets or two free tickets. Check what your own booking includes.

Who it suits, and who should look elsewhere

Hurshimchung suits travelers who want hot-spring water without leaving the subway map, families who need child prices, and anyone caught by a cold or wet day in Busan. Think twice in these cases.

  • You need a bed. Last entry is 21:30 and the bath shuts at 22:00.
  • You have tattoos. We found no published policy. One travel site said in 2024 that tattooed guests are restricted, which we could not confirm. Ask at the counter before you pay.
  • You need help in English. The KTO listing mentions guidance in Korean only.
