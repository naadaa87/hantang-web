title: How to fold the yangmeori, Korea's sheep-head sauna towel description: Three folds turn the small jjimjilbang towel into the "sheep head" from K-dramas. How to make one that stays on, why people wear it, and where the habit started. tag: CULTURE ---

The yangmeori (양머리, "sheep head") is a small towel rolled at both ends and worn like a cap, with one roll above each ear. For many travelers it is the first picture that comes to mind for a jjimjilbang (찜질방), and it takes about thirty seconds to make. Nobody will ask you to wear one. It is just fun, and it does a small job well.

How to fold it

Use one of the small towels you get at the counter. At a large jjimjilbang the ticket usually includes two of them, each close to hand-towel size. A dry towel holds its shape better than a damp one.

  1. Fold it into a strip. Lay the towel flat with a long side toward you and fold it in thirds lengthwise, the way you fold a letter.
  2. Roll both ends. Starting at one short end, roll the strip back on itself toward the middle, the way you roll up a shirt sleeve. Two or three turns are enough. Do the same from the other end, and keep both rolls on the same side of the strip.
  3. Open the middle and put it on. The part left between the rolls is still three layers thick. Open it between the layers like a pouch, pull it over your head like a cap, and let one roll sit above each ear.
IF IT KEEPS SLIDING OFF

Roll each side one more turn so the middle gets shorter, and pull the pouch further down the back of your head. If your hair is long, tie it up first.

Why people wear it

The reason is practical. In the hot rooms your hair gets damp with sweat, and a towel that is only draped over your head slides off every time you lie down or get up. A 2026 explainer by the Korean outlet Wikitree gives exactly these two reasons: the rolled towel keeps sweaty hair in order, and it stays where you put it.

It is not a rule, and it is not old etiquette either. The same article says the yangmeori is not something every Korean does at the jjimjilbang. It became the symbol of the place because TV variety shows and dramas kept using it for a laugh, and visitors began copying it for photos.

Where it came from

We could not find a source that names the person who rolled the first one. What can be dated is the moment it became famous. Korean user-edited wikis credit My Lovely Sam Soon (내 이름은 김삼순), an MBC drama from the summer of 2005 in which the towel appears. A post on the Korean forum 82cook backs up the timing. It is dated 15 August 2005, its title is "Doing the yangmeori after the fad has passed", and it calls the fold "the yangmeori that My Lovely Sam Soon made famous".

The fad did not pass. Newsis wrote in July 2026 that Korean dramas, films and idol videos often show characters folding a towel into a sheep head at the jjimjilbang, and that foreign visitors now shop for Korean sauna goods at Namdaemun Market. In March 2026 the Maeil Business Newspaper described guests at Park Habio Water Kingdom in Songpa, Seoul, learning the fold from the staff and posing for photos. A staff member told the paper that foreign guests who had seen it in Descendants of the Sun asked to be shown how. A Thai visitor in the same story said she put a jjimjilbang on her itinerary after watching members of the idol group NCT WISH visit one on YouTube.

Manners with a towel on your head

  • Wear it in the clothed area. The yangmeori belongs in the shared hot rooms and the lounge, where everyone is in the house uniform. In the bath, towels stay out of the water.
  • Photos only where they are allowed. Phones and cameras are banned in the changing rooms and baths, and filming there is a crime. In the lounge, check the signs and keep strangers out of the frame. The etiquette guide explains the rule.
  • Leave the towel behind. It belongs to the house. Drop it in the used-towel bin on your way out. If you want a souvenir, sauna goods such as the Italy towel scrub mitt are sold at Namdaemun Market, convenience stores and Daiso.
