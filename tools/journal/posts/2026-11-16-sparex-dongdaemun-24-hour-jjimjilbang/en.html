Sparex Dongdaemun (스파렉스 동대문) is a 24-hour jjimjilbang (찜질방) on basement level 3 of the Goodmorning City mall, opposite Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP). One ticket covers the bath, the house uniform and the shared halls for up to 24 hours: ₩14,000 if you enter by day, ₩16,000 at night (as of November 2026). It suits a first visit, a late bath after shopping, or a wait with a suitcase. Our in-person check is still pending, so everything below comes from Seoul's official tourism site, Korean press reports and the ticket terms.

Finding the door: exit 14, Gate 1, elevator to B3

Ride subway Line 2, 4 or 5 to Dongdaemun History & Culture Park Station and leave by exit 14. Goodmorning City is the building in front of you. The ticket listings give the same route: enter through Gate 1 and take the elevator down to B3, because the escalators are not running. From outside, the mall gives no hint of a bathhouse, as VisitSeoul, the city's tourism site, also notes.

Tickets are sold at the counter and at a kiosk with a language menu. With an online voucher, the Seoul Travel Pass listing says to pick your language at the kiosk, scan the QR code, print the ticket and show it to the staff. Your first tang, step by step covers the shoe locker, wristband key and changing room that follow. The uniform and towels come with the ticket. Bring shampoo and conditioner, or buy single-use packets there.

What is inside

The baths are separate for men and women, and nobody wears anything in them. The shared floor, where everyone is in uniform, is the part people photograph. VisitSeoul describes a hall built like a small hanok village, with a pavilion, hanok rafters, lattice doors and an old-style kitchen. The arched hanjeungmak (한증막, a kiln sauna) comes in a medium-heat and a high-heat version, and the ticket listings add clay rooms and an ice room. A food court serves meals such as seaweed soup, plus sikhye (식혜, a sweet rice drink) and eggs. There is a sleeping area, and a gym that any guest may use.

In February 2026 the Korea Economic Daily sent a reporter to a jjimjilbang near exit 14. The article does not name the business, but the location, the hanok-style hall and the luggage service match Sparex. The reporter saw signs in English, Japanese and Chinese at the counter and suitcases beside the shoe lockers. That afternoon the most crowded spot was the cold room; the article also mentions a hot room at 67.5°C. The manager told the paper that most foreign guests come from China, Japan and Southeast Asia.

It closed, and it came back

Old blog posts may tell you Sparex is gone. For a while that was true. In June 2022 the Korean real-estate outlet Ttangjipgo reported that Sparex had closed after a temporary shutdown during the pandemic. In August 2024 a piece by CastPick on the portal Daum said it had reopened and was busy again. Both pieces say the hanok hall became known through the TV travel show 어서와 한국은 처음이지 ("Welcome, is this your first time in Korea?"). We could not confirm the month it reopened or the year it first opened. VisitSeoul's entry, updated in December 2025, lists it as open 24 hours, and tickets were on sale when we checked in November 2026.

These figures are on our Sparex Dongdaemun page (checked October 2026) and matched the Seoul Travel Pass ticket terms in November 2026. Children under 24 months are free, and from age 8 they pay the adult rate. The price board at the counter is the final word.

To sleep there, arrive after 20:00 and buy the night ticket. We could not confirm what is charged when a day ticket runs past 20:00, so ask at the counter. A stay beyond 24 hours costs extra; the listings do not say how much.

WITH A SUITCASE

Normally you cannot leave and come back in. The ₩20,000 ticket is the exception. It adds luggage storage and one trip outside, and it runs for 24 hours from payment. If you stay inside the whole time, the ticket terms say your bags can be stored for free during the visit.

If you stay the night

  • Bathe before midnight. The men's bath closes for cleaning from 00:30 to 01:30 and the women's from 03:00 to 04:00. No scrubs are given between 01:00 and 03:00.
  • Under 19. Minors may not stay between 22:00 and 05:00 without a guardian.
  • Children and the other bath. A boy taller than 105 cm may not go into the women's bath, and the same applies to girls and the men's bath.
  • Alcohol. You may not bring in alcohol or food, and guests who have drunk too much can be turned away.
  • The wristband. Everything you buy inside is charged to it. Losing the key costs ₩20,000.

You sleep in a shared hall on a heated floor, not in a bed. Sleeping overnight in a jjimjilbang explains what that is like and what to pack.

The newer branch at Dongmyo

Sparex Dongmyo (스파렉스 동묘) opened in July 2024 on the 12th and 13th floors of the Season (CIZN) building, directly in front of exit 6 of Dongmyo Station on Lines 1 and 6. The ticket system is the same: day and night split at 20:00, and a luggage ticket allows one trip outside within 24 hours. Three things differ. It sits high above the street, not underground, and the booking page describes a white-and-wood interior, not a hanok set. The baths are cleaned earlier, the men's from 00:00 to 02:00 and the women's from 23:00 to 01:00. And men cannot get a scrub on Monday nights.

Our listing has the day ticket at ₩14,000 (February 2026). The night price we hold, ₩15,000, dates from February 2025, so check the board. Carry your passport, because the branch asked for physical ID in 2025. Tickets for Dongmyo were also on sale in November 2026.

The flea market near exit 3 of the same station sells secondhand clothes, watches, cameras and LPs, according to Seoul's city news site.

Who it suits, and who should look elsewhere

Sparex Dongdaemun makes sense if you are staying nearby, want signs and a kiosk in your language, or need a bath at an hour when other places are shut. It also pairs well with the neighborhood: DDP, the Zaha Hadid building that opened in 2014, stands opposite, and Korea JoongAng Daily wrote in 2018 that Dongdaemun's wholesale fashion market runs until dawn. We did not check each mall's hours, so look them up first.

Think twice in these cases.

  • You sleep lightly. Expect light and snoring in a shared hall. For real rest, a hotel is better.
  • You have tattoos. We found no published policy, so ask at the counter. Tattoos in Korean saunas explains how.
  • You are driving. Parking is free for three hours if you register at the desk before leaving, then ₩1,500 per 30 minutes. Overnight, that adds up.
  • You are coming on a holiday. Staff told the Korea Economic Daily that foreign guests more than doubled over the 2026 Lunar New Year, by their own estimate.
  • You want daylight. The whole place is three floors below ground. Our listing for the Dongmyo branch mentions a lounge that faces Namsan Tower.
