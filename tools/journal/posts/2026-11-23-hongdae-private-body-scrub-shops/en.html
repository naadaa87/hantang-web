title: Private Korean scrub shops in Hongdae: what you get, how to book description: A one-person scrub shop gives you a private room, your own tub and one therapist. How four shops near Hongdae work, what they cost in November 2026, and who is better off at a bathhouse. tag: PLACES ---

A one-person scrub shop (1인 세신샵, irin seshin shop) sells the Korean body scrub, seshin (세신), without the public bath around it. You book a time, soak in your own tub in a private room, and one therapist scrubs you. At the three shops near Hongdae that publish prices in won, sessions run 50 to 120 minutes and cost ₩87,000 to ₩258,000 (November 2026). A scrub at an ordinary bathhouse cost ₩20,000 to ₩40,000 in August 2025, according to Korea JoongAng Daily. Three of the four shops below take women only. Our in-person check is still pending, so the facts below come from VisitSeoul (the city's official tourism site), government license data, Korean press and the shops' booking pages.

The work itself is the same: softened skin and a rough mitt, as our seshin guide describes. What you pay for is the room and the time. The booking platform Creatrip puts a basic 60-minute private scrub at ₩80,000 to ₩150,000 in its 2026 guide.

What happens in the room

Korea JoongAng Daily's August 2025 report, which featured DAMDA, describes a session. Before the bath, the guest fills in a short form: which tea, which bath product, how hard a scrub. Then come about 15 minutes in the tub and about 30 minutes of scrubbing, followed by a milk rinse, a massage, a facial, and a shampoo with a scalp massage. SEIAN's booking page lists much the same in every course: a 15-minute bath with Dead Sea salt, the scrub, a milk wash, a mask pack, a shampoo and a hair pack. THE SEOL:HA gives guests 20 minutes in a private bath.

Only THE SEOL:HA's page says what you wear: disposable underwear is provided. Elsewhere, ask when you book. If the scrub hurts, say 살살 해주세요 (salsal haejuseyo, "gently, please").

DAMDA

DAMDA (세신샵 담다) is the only one of the four with an entry on VisitSeoul, last updated on November 6, 2026. It opens daily from 07:00 to 22:00, except on the days of Seollal and Chuseok. VisitSeoul says menus are ready in English and Japanese. Korea JoongAng Daily reported that the shop opened in April 2025 with three private rooms, each with its own changing room and bathroom, and that foreigners made up 20 to 30 percent of guests at the time.

Klook sells 80-, 100- and 120-minute packages. The shop confirms within 24 hours and offers the nearest slot or a full refund if your time is taken. One caution: the ₩83,000 starting price from 2025, which our listing page still shows, is out of date.

SEIAN Yeonnam and SEIAN Donggyo

SEIAN (세이안) has two women-only branches, in Yeonnam-dong and Donggyo-dong. SEIAN Yeonnam lists seven courses, from the 50-minute Milk Course to the 110-minute Seian Special. SEIAN Donggyo adds a choice of scent and a 120-minute course at ₩258,000. You book a time on Creatrip and choose the course at the shop. Changes are free up to three days ahead, and confirmation takes one to two days. The pages list English and Japanese, and Creatrip's guide says sanitary pads and tampons are available.

We found no tourism-board entry or press report for SEIAN. The license data dates the Yeonnam branch to 2024 and Donggyo to 2025, and both booking pages were up today with full menus. They carry no dated notice and no opening hours. Our Yeonnam listing records last reservations at 21:30.

THE SEOL:HA

THE SEOL:HA (더 설하) is the exception: it is not women only. Its Trazy page says male guests are treated by male therapists, and a couple room can be requested, in which only the 20-minute bath is shared. It sells five scrub courses alongside head spa and massage menus, and opens daily from 09:00 to 21:00, with the last reservation at 19:00 to 19:30. Its Creatrip page says the staff speak English, Japanese and Chinese.

On the figures we hold, it is the most expensive of the four, and its terms are the strictest: a full refund only until 17:00 Korean time three days ahead, and none within two days. The shop was licensed in 2026, and its Trazy page was open for booking today. We found no tourism-board entry or press report for it either.

How to book, and what to ask first

  • Book ahead. DAMDA's page says it is 100% pre-booked. Allow a day or two for confirmation, and read the cancellation terms: Klook's DAMDA page does not state any.
  • Mention your skin. DAMDA asks guests to say in advance if they have skin problems or wounds. Our seshin guide lists who should skip a scrub altogether.
  • Ask about tattoos. None of the pages we read mentions them. A private room has no other bathers, but that is our reasoning, not a published policy.
  • Be on time. THE SEOL:HA asks guests to arrive 15 minutes early and may shorten the session of anyone who is late.

Who it suits, and who should go to a bathhouse

A one-person shop suits you if being naked among strangers is what has kept you out of a bathhouse, if you want the steps explained in English or Japanese, or if your itinerary needs a fixed appointment. Hongik University Station is on the Airport Railroad, so an arrival or departure day works too.

Look elsewhere in these cases.

  • You are watching your budget. The cheapest course here costs more than double the top of the bathhouse range.
  • You want the bathhouse itself. A shop gives you one tub in one room. The pools, saunas and shared lounge of a jjimjilbang (찜질방) are not part of it. Bathhouses near Hongdae lists both kinds.
  • You are a man. Of these four, only THE SEOL:HA takes men. For most men the bathhouse scrub is still the usual route.
